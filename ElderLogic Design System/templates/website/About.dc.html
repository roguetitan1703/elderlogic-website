About · a note from the founder

I am the person who makes the calls.

I spent years in hospice placement in Arizona, and the same thing happened every week. A patient needed somewhere to live by Friday, and the search started from whichever homes somebody could remember.

That is not a failure of effort. There are around 2,600 licensed senior living homes in this state. Nobody holds that in their head, and nothing in a hospice team's day makes room for calling homes that have never come up before. So the same twelve get called, and the ones with an open room this week never hear from anyone.

I built the database first, out of the state's own record. Every licensed home, with its licensing, inspection, violation and enforcement history from AZDHS, refreshed every month. That part is software and it works.

The rest is not software, and I want to be plain about that. When a search comes in, somebody reads the record on those homes, picks up the phone, works out who has space for this patient this week, and agrees what it will cost. Right now that somebody is usually me, and as we grow it will be people I train. A hospice team gets a shortlist of homes that will take the patient. They do not get a list of two hundred addresses and good luck.

One thing I will not do is tell you a home is nice. I am not in a position to judge that and neither is a piece of software. The state publishes what it found when it inspected, and ElderLogic puts that in front of you with a link to the original document. You decide. That rule is not a feature, it is the reason the data can be trusted at all.

Everything else in the product comes from watching liaisons work. Routes, because a day of visits is driving. Short forms, because nobody fills a long one standing in a car park. Sending the route to the family, because families were being handed three addresses and left to work out the order themselves.

If you run a hospice in Arizona and you want to see what is actually in your territory, book twenty minutes. I will open the map on your area and you can look at it yourself.

Founder, ElderLogic

Phoenix, Arizona
