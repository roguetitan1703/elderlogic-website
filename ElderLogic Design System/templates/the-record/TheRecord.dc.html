Hospice placement and territory work · Arizona

Your liaisons know twelve homes in Mesa.

Arizona has around 2,600 licensed senior living homes. The rest of the territory is invisible: homes with open rooms, homes that would refer, homes nobody has ever called.

ElderLogic holds all of them, with the state's own licensing and inspection record on each one. Then a person on our team does the calling. That combination is the product.

What happens, in order

One placement, start to finish. No sales argument in this section.

  1. 01

    An assessment is filled in

    It creates the client record, the power-of-attorney record and the placement search in one pass. Only critical fields are required.

  2. 02

    The search returns every licensed home in range

    Around 200 of them, each with its AZDHS licensing, inspection, violation and enforcement history. Homes your organisation has blacklisted are already gone.

  3. 03

    Our team screens them and makes the calls

    We read the state record, contact the homes, confirm who has a room for this patient, and agree the price.

  4. 04

    You receive a shortlist that will take the patient

    Five or so homes with a confirmed room and an agreed price.

  5. 05

    The shortlist becomes a routed day

    Pre-tour visits in order on a phone, re-optimised when the day changes, with a short form at each stop. The same route can be sent to the family to open in Google Maps.

  6. 06

    The move-in is recorded

    Which client, which home, what price, what date. A home that failed its visit stays out of every future search.

The same map runs territory work: select an area, get every licensed home inside it in a routed order. It does not replace an EMR or a CRM.

This is the territory, not a sample of it.

Homes decide which hospice gets called when a resident starts declining. A liaison can only build that relationship with homes they know exist. Every pin here carries contact details and the full state record, and any shape drawn around them becomes a day of visits.

Why territory pays

A referral from assisted living stays on service longer.

Patients referred from assisted living average around 150 lifetime days on hospice. Referrals from home average around 87. Coverage of the homes in your territory is the input to that number, and census is what an owner is measured on.

Before a visit, we contact the homes on the route with your placement pitch, so a liaison arrives somewhere that already wants the meeting: a resident who might fit, and no placement agent fee for the home to pay.

In the field

The day arrives as a route, not a list of addresses.

Stops in order, re-optimised when the day changes. Each one opens that home's answers for this client, the key AZDHS history, and one click to the official file. The form afterwards is short, and every field on it is optional, because a long form never gets filled in a car park.

The same route can go to the family by text or email and opens in Google Maps.
