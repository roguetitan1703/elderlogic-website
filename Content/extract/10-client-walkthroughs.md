# Client walkthroughs — recordings 15 and 16

Source: Standard recording 15 and 16, 23 July 2026. The client demonstrating the product and talking through it unscripted.
Extraction only. Verbatim where quoted. **Her language is blunt and occasionally profane — most of it is source, not copy.**

**Why this file matters more than the other nine:** every deck in `extract/` is us writing about the product. This is her talking about it, without a slide. It is the only recording of the voice the site is supposed to sound like, it contains two pieces of content no deck has, and it corrects two things we had assumed.

---

## 1. What the recordings confirm

The flow, as she demonstrates it:

1. Dashboard opens on the map — "these are all the homes, these are the facilities"
2. Assessment form: "all the client and POA information"
3. Submitting it "automatically makes a client record and a power of attorney record and a placement search record, and triggers a Mapsly search"
4. The search "kicks back all the homes in that area they wanted to be in — they want to be at 123 Main, five mile radius. All that happens automatically."
5. Placement search record + outreach logs, per client
6. She screens, contacts, negotiates: room availability and price
7. Pre-tour built for the homes that work — a navigation route
8. At each stop: navigate, the AZDHS link, the post-visit form
9. Form has "add to family tour"
10. Family route emailed or texted, opens in Google Maps, same route
11. Move-in record: "this client moved into this home for this much money on this date. That's it. And then done."

This matches the six-step flow in the decks. Nothing in the written material contradicts the demo.

## 2. What the recordings add that no deck has

### 2.1 The blacklist story

> "I went to a pre-tour and got charged by three German shepherds, with folks in there on walkers, and the guy was smoking next to an oxygen tank. So obviously — blacklisted."

One anecdote that justifies the pre-tour visit, the short form, and the blacklist at the same time. It is the only piece of storytelling in any source material, it is true, and it is hers. Nothing in nine decks comes close.

### 2.2 What the post-visit form actually asks

> "It's not required fields. It's just like, hey — does it smell like shit? Hey, were people smoking in there? Does anybody speak English? Do you want to blacklist this home?"

The decks say "the form is short and optional." They never say what it asks. The real questions are the reason it is short, and they are the reason a rep fills it in.

### 2.3 Why homes matter, told as a scene

> "I'm a home owner. I have a dude that's dying and I need a hospice to come in and help him die. Well, there's a trillion hospice companies."

Our decks flatten this to *"homes decide which hospice gets called when a resident starts declining."* Hers is the same fact with a person in it. The home is choosing between competitors, and the rep who never showed up does not get chosen.

### 2.4 Her name for the two use cases

> "It's a placement tool, but it's also a marketing tool."

Stated plainly, by her, once. Not in any deck. This is the sentence that joins the two halves of the product.

### 2.5 The second use case was discovered, not designed

> "A use case that I didn't even think of at all… So yes, there's the original use case that we built this thing for. But what John had told me is that hospice reps are going to homes to try to get their business."

Territory work was not on the roadmap. A customer's reality produced it. That is a credible story about a product that pays attention.

### 2.6 The coverage gap, in her voice

> "They have no idea. They're like, oh — I know twelve homes in Mesa."
> "It's wildly irresponsible to not know where all the homes are. But they don't."
> "They don't know their phone numbers, they don't know their email addresses, they don't know their history. They can't go introduce themselves and try to get business."

The hook we have been using in eight decks originates here, and the spoken version is sharper than every written version of it.

### 2.7 The uncomfortable truth behind the reporting add-on

> "They're just like at home acting like they're working, but they're not working."

This is what visit verification is actually for. Unpublishable as written — it insults the buyer's staff. Worth knowing that the add-on has a real reason.

## 3. Two corrections to what we assumed

### 3.1 The area-selection lasso — RESOLVED, no longer an issue

> "In Mapsly — because for some reason I don't see it in the dashboard, I need to message Mohim… they do this little lasso tool, which is so cool. And they go, I mean, that's like ninety homes. They choose all those homes and then it builds a route for them just like the pre-tour thing."

**Since fixed.** Confirmed by the client: the app is working correctly and area selection is in the dashboard. This closes open question 9 in the positioning document — the feature is real, it is in the product, and it can be written about and captured.

### 3.2 The AZDHS panel did not load in the demo

> "If I click on this, it's supposed to tell me all the AZDHS stuff, but I can see that it's not doing that. So I'll have to fix it."

The state record is the credibility argument on five of nine decks. In the walkthrough it did not render. Same category as 3.1 — a July QA state, not a product limit. Confirm it renders before the screenshot is taken.

**Read these recordings as raw material, not as a status report.** She was talking freely to give us the detail and the vibe. Turning it into something marketable is our job, not hers — she says so herself: *"I'm too deep in it and can't see the macro vision."*

## 4. The funnel, from her mouth

She gives it twice in one call, and the two do not match.

**First telling — reading the placement search record:**
> "This would say like two hundred in the neighborhood, forty blacklisted, we contacted fifty."

**Second telling — narrating the pre-tour:**
> "There were two hundred. We contacted sixty, twenty replied, five we pre-toured, and then two we're actually going to take the family to."

The published funnel across the decks is the second version. So the numbers are hers, not invented by us — but she stated them twice, differently, in one sitting, and the first version contains a step the decks drop entirely: **40 blacklisted before contact**. That step is a better argument than any single row we currently publish, because it is the customer's own knowledge doing work.

Neither telling is sourced to a real search. Still needs confirming.

- "They're at home acting like they're working."
