# ElderLogic Design System Website A

Source: `Website options/ElderLogic Design System Website A.pdf`
Extraction only. No verdicts. Copy is verbatim.

**Deck's own idea:** comparison A's coverage-gap hook, rebuilt against the design system and made calmer. The hero splits into problem (left) and answer (right). The six-step flow is stated as a plain numbered list with an explicit promise not to argue inside it. Shortest of the DS builds.

---

## S0 — Header

- **Nav:** How it works · Coverage · About
- **CTA:** Book a demo

## S1 — Hero

- **Eyebrow:** HOSPICE PLACEMENT AND TERRITORY WORK — ARIZONA
- **Heading:** Your liaisons know twelve homes in Mesa.
- **Body left (the problem):** Arizona has around 2,600 licensed senior living homes. The rest of the territory is invisible: homes with open rooms, homes that would refer, homes nobody has ever called.
- **Body right (the answer):** ElderLogic holds all of them, with the state's own licensing and inspection record on each one. Then a person on our team does the calling. That combination is the product.
- **CTA (primary):** Book a demo
- **CTA (secondary):** Read how it works
- **Asset ask:** none stated
- **Topic:** coverage gap, with the product named in the same breath
- **Note:** "That combination is the product" is the only line in any deck that states the software-plus-people pairing as the thing being sold

## S2 — What happens, in order

- **Eyebrow:** PLAINLY
- **Heading:** What happens, in order
- **Sub-heading:** One placement, start to finish. No sales argument in this section.

| # | Step | Body |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | An assessment is filled in | It creates the client record, the power-of-attorney record and the placement search in one pass. Only critical fields are required. |
| 02 | The search returns every licensed home in range | Around 200 of them, each with its AZDHS licensing, inspection, violation and enforcement history. Homes your organisation has blacklisted are already gone. |
| 03 | Our team screens them and makes the calls | We read the state record, contact the homes, confirm who has a room for this patient, and agree the price. |
| 04 | You receive a shortlist that will take the patient | Five or so homes with a confirmed room and an agreed price. |
| 05 | The shortlist becomes a routed day | Pre-tour visits in order on a phone, re-optimised when the day changes, with a short form at each stop. The same route can be sent to the family to open in Google Maps. |
| 06 | The move-in is recorded | Which client, which home, what price, what date. A home that failed its visit stays out of every future search. |

- **Closing line:** The same map runs territory work: select an area, get every licensed home inside it in a routed order. It does not replace an EMR or a CRM.
- **Asset ask:** none
- **Topic:** how it works
- **Note:** the funnel numbers are folded into the steps here (200 in step 02, five in step 04) rather than given their own graphic

## S3 — The territory

- **Heading:** This is the territory, not a sample of it.
- **Body:** Homes decide which hospice gets called when a resident starts declining. A liaison can only build that relationship with homes they know exist. Every pin here carries contact details and the full state record, and any shape drawn around them becomes a day of visits.
- **Caption:** Licensed homes, Phoenix metro
- **Source line:** Source: AZDHS — refreshed monthly — ~2,600 statewide as of Aug 2026
- **Asset ask:** Phoenix metro map, pin density
- **Topic:** territory / coverage

## S4 — Why territory pays

- **Eyebrow:** WHY TERRITORY PAYS
- **Heading:** A referral from assisted living stays on service longer.
- **Body:** Patients referred from assisted living average around 150 lifetime days on hospice. Referrals from home average around 87. Coverage of the homes in your territory is the input to that number, and census is what an owner is measured on.
- **Stat 1:** 150 — average lifetime days — referred from assisted living
- **Stat 2:** 87 — average lifetime days — referred from home
- **Warm-introduction paragraph:** Before a visit, we contact the homes on the route with your placement pitch, so a liaison arrives somewhere that already wants the meeting: a resident who might fit, and no placement agent fee for the home to pay.
- **Asset ask:** none stated
- **Topic:** the owner's number / warm introductions

## S5 — In the field

- **Eyebrow:** IN THE FIELD
- **Heading:** The day arrives as a route, not a list of addresses.
- **Body:** Stops in order, re-optimised when the day changes. Each one opens that home's answers for this client, the key AZDHS history, and one click to the official file. The form afterwards is short, and every field on it is optional, because a long form never gets filled in a car park.
- **Closing line:** The same route can go to the family by text or email and opens in Google Maps.
- **Image captions:** Pre-tour route · Territory visit setup
- **Asset ask:** two phone screens — pre-tour route, territory visit setup
- **Topic:** field workflow / family route

## S6 — Close

- **Heading:** Bring your territory to the demo.
- **Body:** We open the map on the area your liaisons cover and you can see what is in it.
- **Reserved:** RESERVED — CALENDLY EMBED · Scheduling
- **CTA:** Book a demo
- **Asset ask:** Calendly embed
- **Topic:** demo offer
