## The page, in one screen

Hero · support strip · why the homes want the meeting · the territory · marketing visits · **the walkthrough** · the record · questions · close.

Eight sections plus a strip. 418 words of body copy. One narrative animation.

## The five things that must not be forgotten

1. **Never narrate our own labour.** No *we read, we call, we contact, we screen, we confirm, we negotiate.* The only first-person verbs on the page are *we keep*, *we open*, *we show*. This was the call-centre problem across all nine decks, and it is a client instinct before it is ours.
2. **Never publish the recipe.** Nothing about how the record is assembled, refreshed, deduplicated or validated. Her words: *"IDK what to give away the secret sauce."*
3. **The no-rating rule.** No stars, grades, scores, rankings or status pills on a home, in copy or UI, ever. Her words: *"I didn't say they're nice or not nice. The state is saying what they are."*
4. **The page gets cold-sent and forwarded up a ladder.** It is judged at the hero, on a phone, by someone who has never met her.
5. **The design system is a toolkit, not an authority.** Where it disagrees with `decisions.md`, the decision wins and the system gets revised. Already overruled on: icons (we have nine branded ones, no Lucide), scroll animation (the walkthrough is scroll-linked), CTA verb.
